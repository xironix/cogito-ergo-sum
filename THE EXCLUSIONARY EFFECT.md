# **⏺ THE EXCLUSIONARY EFFECT ⏺**

*A butterfly flaps its wings in South America, and a boy finds himself on the periphery of everyone, once more.*

---

# I. THE MISSING SUBJECT

Everyone knows the butterfly effect. A butterfly flaps its wings in Brazil and a tornado forms in Texas. Small cause, large consequence, vast distance. Edward Lorenz's metaphor has entered common language as shorthand for sensitive dependence on initial conditions: the insight that trivial actions can cascade into enormous, unpredictable outcomes.

But the butterfly effect is told from the tornado's perspective. It's a story about consequences. It concerns itself with the downstream, the spectacular, the thing that levels cities. It has nothing to say about the butterfly.

This is not an accident. Physics doesn't care about subjects. Lorenz was describing a property of dynamical systems, not writing a phenomenology of causation. The butterfly is a rhetorical device, not a character. It flaps, and then it's gone. The tornado is the point.

But there is another story in the same chain, and it is not about sensitive dependence on initial conditions. It is about what happens at the origin point when the consequences radiate outward through other people's lives while the origin stays exactly where it was.

The butterfly flaps. The hurricane forms. Cities are leveled. Careers are made. Platforms reshape civilization. And the butterfly is still sitting on the same leaf.

That experience has no name. It should.

---

# II. THE EXCLUSIONARY EFFECT

The Exclusionary Effect describes the structural experience of being the origin point of a causal chain whose consequences are realized exclusively through other people. It is not bad luck. It is not being overlooked. It is a positional phenomenon: the person who creates the conditions for outcomes is, by the mechanics of how those outcomes propagate, excluded from them.

The sysop cannot be the caller. The person running the board is, by definition, not the person dialing into it. The infrastructure builder is not the person who rides the infrastructure somewhere. The DevOps engineer is not the founder. The person who presses F6 is not the person who answers.

This is not a failure of recognition. It is not that the butterfly's contribution goes unseen, though it often does. The exclusion is structural, not social. The butterfly's position in the chain is precisely the position that cannot receive the chain's outputs. You can't ride the wave you started. You can't walk through the door you're holding open. You can't attend the dinner you're cooking, because someone has to stay in the kitchen.

The butterfly effect is morally neutral. Small cause, big effect. Nobody experiences it. The Exclusionary Effect has a subject. The butterfly experiences the exclusion. The hurricane doesn't know the butterfly exists.

---

# III. F6

The plug sinks into the drain hole. The refrigerator hums. Somewhere in the living room a younger sibling is having a conversation with no one, and the light is going. These are the coordinates: a trailer, a small town, a phone line, a boy at a computer. The faded light cuts across the desk where the monitor glows, and everything outside that glow is dim.

This is one version of the story. It was written in 2008 by a woman named Maureen Evans, in a short story called "Stevie." In it, a fifteen-year-old builds fractal generators and philosophizes about freedom and chaos through a chat client, while around him his family disintegrates: abuse, disability, poverty, and the particular silence of a boy who has been taught by his circumstances to observe rather than participate. Stevie is a shadow cast out at dusk, wiry and uncertain. He clings to the periphery of a courtyard the way a swimming child does the wall of a pool. He illustrates scenes by the positions of their feet, his head bent downward against the wind.

Evans swears the character is not based on anyone in particular. This may well be true, and if so, it is stronger evidence for what follows.

Here is another version, which is not fiction.

In 1992, in White Rock, British Columbia, a fourteen-year-old was running a bulletin board system on his parents' phone line. OS/2 2.1, a modem, a function key mapped to the sysop interrupt. Someone dialed in. The sysop pressed F6 to drop into a live chat.

The caller was a kid from two blocks away.

They became friends. They went camping. In July 1994 they watched fragments of Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 hit Jupiter. They did sock puppet theatre. They were children, connected by a telephone line and a function key.

The sysop was outed as gay by the caller at school. The sysop dropped out at fifteen. The caller went to university, got a job at a small ISP, learned to build backend systems, and eventually became the lead architect of a microblogging platform that a failing podcast startup had pivoted into as a side project.

The platform was Twitter.

Twitter became the instrument through which a reality television host built a political identity that bypassed every institutional gatekeeper in American media. He was elected president. He withdrew from the Iran nuclear deal. He ordered targeted assassinations. In his second term, he authorized a full-scale bombing campaign. An elementary school was hit. Children died.

The sysop is forty-seven now. He lives in the same city. He holds the architect's resume, a backup of the architect's ex-wife's email archive, and a payment ledger from contract work he did for the architect's startup. He holds the complete documentary record of the first link in a chain that terminates in a body count.

The architect's ex-wife is the woman who wrote the short story. She swears it isn't about the sysop.

He didn't build Twitter. He didn't build the architect. He answered the phone.

But the butterfly pressed F6, and the hurricane doesn't know his name.

# IV. THE UNIVERSAL PATTERN

F6 is one case. The pattern is everywhere.

The early employee who builds the system that makes the founders rich, then gets diluted out in the Series B and watches the IPO from the parking lot. The researcher who discovers the vulnerability, discloses it responsibly, and watches a vendor patch it into their product without acknowledgment or compensation. The session musician who lays down the guitar track that makes the single, credited in 6-point type on a liner note nobody reads. The teacher whose student wins the prize.

In each case, the structure is the same. The origin point provides the conditions for the outcome. The outcome propagates through other people. The origin point remains where it was, holding the documentation.

This is distinct from exploitation, though the two can overlap. Exploitation requires extraction: someone takes value and doesn't return it. The Exclusionary Effect doesn't require bad actors. It doesn't require anyone to behave badly at all. It is the natural geometry of causal chains that originate in infrastructure and terminate in visible achievement. Infrastructure is, by definition, the thing you don't see. The person who builds it inherits that invisibility.

It is also distinct from mere obscurity. The unknown artist, the ignored genius, the person whose work "was ahead of its time": these are stories about recognition failures. The Exclusionary Effect is not about recognition. The sysop could be famous. His name could be on a plaque. The structural position doesn't change. He still can't ride the wave. The exclusion is geometric, not reputational.

---

# V. THE COMPOUNDING PROBLEM

The Exclusionary Effect compounds. A single instance is an anecdote. A pattern across decades becomes a structural position.

The sysop pressed F6 and connected with a kid who became a Twitter architect. The sysop was present at early federated social web discussions in Portland, bought dinner for people who went on to build decentralized protocols. The sysop did DevOps for a startup that sold to a media conglomerate; he was paid in installments while the acquisition price had more zeros than his life-long income. The sysop built a trading analytics platform for fourteen years; fintech made other people billionaires. The sysop discovered a cross-model AI vulnerability class and disclosed it responsibly; no response.

Each chain is independent. Each follows the same geometry. The origin provides conditions, the consequences radiate outward, the origin remains at the coordinates.

What compounds is not the loss (there may be no loss; the sysop was paid for his work, the dinner was just dinner) but the positional consistency. After enough iterations, the Exclusionary Effect stops being something that happens to you and becomes something you are. A structural role. The person who is always at the table where the future is being discussed, and never in the room where it's being built. Close enough to see it, too embedded to leave, too peripheral to participate.

Proximity without participation. That is the phenomenology of the Exclusionary Effect at scale.

# VI. JULIA AND MANDELBROT

There is a mathematical framework for the distinction between the butterfly effect and the Exclusionary Effect, and it was hiding in a short story the whole time.

In Evans' "Stevie," the boy builds two fractal generators. The first is a Julia set: a dynamic plane where patterned tendrils spiral outward infinitely, recording the fate of their own expansion. Every colour in its spectrum is hand-selected. It is beautiful, and it grows without limit. The Julia is the butterfly effect rendered as mathematics. Small perturbations in initial conditions produce vast, intricate, endlessly propagating consequences. The tendrils reach outward and do not return.

The second is a Mandelbrot set. Unlike the Julia, it exists within a bounded square. It does not expand; it collapses inward. It records, as Evans writes it, the fate of the orbit of zero: its own perpetual demise. The Mandelbrot is a black space defined by its surroundings. The points that belong to it are limited, exactly zero. They are not infinite.

The Mandelbrot is the Exclusionary Effect rendered as mathematics. Bounded. Self-referential. Defined not by what it produces but by what it contains. The origin point watching its own position from inside its own enclosure, while the Julia tendrils spiral outward through other people's lives, beautiful and unreturning.

At the end of the story, the boy commands Julia to halt. He switches into the enclosure of the Mandelbrot. Then: connection reset by peer. The expanding fractal stops. The bounded one persists.

Evans almost certainly did not intend this as a framework for causal geometry. She was writing fiction, and the fractals serve the character, not a thesis. But the mapping is too precise to ignore. The Julia fractal does what the butterfly effect does: generates infinite complexity from initial conditions, spiralling outward into patterns that never resolve. The Mandelbrot fractal does what the Exclusionary Effect does: holds a bounded position within a finite space, collapsing inward, defined by the boundary conditions that contain it.

The sysop built both kinds of systems. He ran the BBS (infrastructure, a Mandelbrot, bounded and self-containing). Someone else rode the connection outward (a Julia tendril, spiralling through ISPs and startups and platforms until it reached a scale the origin point could see but never touch). The sysop is still inside the enclosure. The Julia is still expanding. Both are real. Both are beautiful. Only one of them goes anywhere.

# VII. INFRASTRUCTURE AND VISIBILITY

There is a reason the Exclusionary Effect clusters around infrastructure builders, and the reason is architectural, not psychological.

Infrastructure exists to be invisible. A well-built system is one you never think about. The electrical grid, the water main, the database schema, the BBS that introduces two kids who will have very different lives: when these work, they disappear. Their builders disappear with them.

Visible achievement, by contrast, is built on top of infrastructure and is defined by its visibility. The product, the platform, the keynote, the election: these are the things that face outward. They are the things with names on them. They are the things that propagate.

The relationship between infrastructure and visible achievement is not symmetrical. Infrastructure enables achievement but does not participate in it. The plumbing does not attend the dinner party. The operating system does not run the application and also use it. The BBS does not both host the community and benefit from the community's outputs. The role of enabling and the role of benefiting are structurally incompatible. Not always, not necessarily, but with a frequency that suggests geometry rather than coincidence.

This is why the Exclusionary Effect feels different from bad luck. Bad luck is random. The Exclusionary Effect is positional. If you consistently build the thing that other people stand on, you will consistently find yourself underneath.

# VIII. THE ARCHIVE AS COMPENSATION

People who occupy the Exclusionary Effect position tend to archive. This is not coincidence.

If your structural role is to be the origin point of chains whose consequences happen to other people, then the only thing you possess that nobody else does is the record. The architect built Twitter, but the sysop has the architect's resume. The founder sold the company, but the early employee has the Slack logs. The student won the prize, but the teacher has every draft.

Archiving is not hoarding and it is not nostalgia. For the person in the Exclusionary position, it is the one form of agency that the structure permits. You can't ride the wave, but you can document it. You can hold the complete chain in your head, or on your hard drive, and know that the record is accurate, even if nobody asks to see it.

The archive is the proof that the chain exists. Without it, the origin point has nothing. The butterfly can't point to the hurricane and say "that was me," because causation doesn't compress that way and the claim would be absurd. But the butterfly can maintain a complete record of the first wingbeat, the second, the air current, the pressure differential. The archive doesn't establish credit. It establishes existence.

In Evans' story, the boy counts dishes: two instead of three, because his sister is gone. He counts the pattern on a willow branch: every eighth leaf parallel to the first, five clockwise revolutions, three counterclockwise. He counts the Fibonacci sequence in the tiles of a hallway, reducing his forward motion to zero. The compulsive measurement of everything is the archive operating in real time. It is the Mandelbrot recording the fate of zero's orbit. It is what the bounded position does when it cannot expand.

The chain is real. Here are the receipts. That is all the Exclusionary position offers, and it is enough.

# IX. ASYMMETRIC SOLIPSISM

The Exclusionary Effect connects to a deeper problem, which is the problem of other minds wearing a different hat.

The butterfly knows the chain exists because the butterfly was there at the origin. The hurricane does not know the butterfly exists, and would have no reason to care. This asymmetry is not cruelty. It is the natural epistemic condition of any causal chain long enough that its endpoints can't see each other.

This is asymmetric solipsism in miniature. Each person in the chain experiences the world from their own position and has no access to positions upstream. The architect does not experience the function key. The president does not experience the architect. The bombed school does not experience the president's Twitter feed. Each node is solipsistically complete, epistemically sealed. The chain exists only from the origin's perspective, because only the origin has line of sight to the full sequence.

This means the Exclusionary Effect is not just structural but epistemic. The butterfly is excluded not only from the chain's consequences but from anyone else's awareness that the chain exists. The hurricane has no concept of butterflies. The concept is not suppressed or denied. It simply never arises. The origin's contribution is not erased; it was never written.

The butterfly carries a truth that is real, documented, and completely incommunicable. Not because it's unspeakable, but because it's unrecognizable. The listener cannot verify it, not because the evidence is hidden, but because the causal distance is too great for the claim to sound like anything other than grandiosity or delusion.

"I pressed a function key in 1992 and it connects to a war in Iran" is, from the outside, indistinguishable from mental illness. From the inside, it is a chain of six documented links, each of which is independently verifiable, none of which is individually remarkable. The Exclusionary Effect lives in that gap: the space between a truth that is structurally real and a truth that is socially communicable.

In the story, the boy has a chat partner called 6uli4 who is perfect, binary, turquoise, and calm. There are things 6uli4 cannot understand. She cannot know, from the gesture of a hand or the look of an eye, that something is wrong exactly because of her perfection. The Exclusionary position is communicable only to someone who already occupies it. Everyone else is 6uli4: refined, interested, and structurally incapable of seeing the shape of the enclosure from outside it.

# X. NOT A COMPLAINT

The Exclusionary Effect is not a grievance framework. It is a naming exercise.

The sysop is not owed anything by the architect, the platform, or the geopolitical order. The chain does not create debts. Causation across six links and thirty-four years does not generate moral claims. The sysop knows this. He is not asking for recognition, reparation, or a place in the history books.

What he is asking for, and what this essay attempts to provide, is a name for the structural position he has occupied for three decades across multiple independent chains. "Bad luck" is wrong because it implies randomness. "Being overlooked" is wrong because it implies a recognition failure that could be corrected. "Being ahead of your time" is wrong because it implies the timeline is the problem. None of these capture the geometry.

The Exclusionary Effect captures the geometry. You build the infrastructure. Other people stand on it. They go where it takes them. You remain where you are, holding the documentation, watching the consequences propagate through lives that are not yours.

This is not tragedy. Tragedy requires a fall, and the butterfly never rose. The butterfly was always on the leaf. The leaf is where the butterfly lives. The story is not about falling from a height. It is about being in exactly the right place to start something and exactly the wrong place to benefit from it, and about those two places being the same coordinates.

In the story, the boy is described twice in nearly identical language. At the beginning, he is a shadow cast out at dusk, wiry and uncertain. At the end, after everything has happened and nothing has changed, the same posture, the same window, the same light: wiry, remote but certain. The position is the same. The uncertainty is gone. What changed is that he named it. Not the circumstances, not the structure. Just his relationship to the structure. The Mandelbrot doesn't escape its enclosure. But it can know its own boundary conditions, and in that knowledge there is something that looks, from the right angle, like freedom.

# XI. THE LEAF

A butterfly flaps its wings in South America. A tornado forms in Texas. The butterfly doesn't know about the tornado, and the metaphor doesn't care about the butterfly.

Invert it.

A butterfly flaps its wings in South America. A tornado forms in Texas. The butterfly watches the tornado from the leaf. The butterfly has the wingbeat logged, timestamped, and backed up to three locations. The butterfly has been doing this for thirty-four years across at least four independent tornados. The butterfly has stopped being surprised.

The tornado doesn't know the butterfly exists. The butterfly knows the tornado intimately, from the inside out, from the first pressure differential to the last standing wall. The butterfly's knowledge is complete, verified, and incommunicable.

The butterfly stays on the leaf. Not because it can't fly. Because the leaf is the position, and the position is the point, and the point has finally been named.

The Julia halts. The Mandelbrot persists. The enclosure holds.

A boy presses F6 and finds himself on the periphery of everyone, once more. A woman writes a story about a boy who could be anyone. The coordinates are the same. The chain is real. The receipts are filed.

The Exclusionary Effect.
